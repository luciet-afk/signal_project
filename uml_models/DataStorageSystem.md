The UML represents three core classes: DataStorage, DataRetriever, and PatientData. Each of these classes are further supported by Vitals, AccessControl, and RetentionPolicy. PatientData serves as the fundamental unit of storage, encapsulating a patient identifier, a timestamp, a version number, and a snapshot of their Vitals at that point in time. DataStorage acts as the central storage interface, responsible solely for storing and deleting records. Deletion is handled carefully, as to delete the data meaningfully. Specifically, the deletion is delegated to the RetenionPolicy, which encapsulates the timeframe of how long data can be kept before it is scrubbed. This separates the concern of what to delete from when and why, keeping DataStorage focused on a single responsibility. Retrieval is handled entirely by DataRetriever, which holds a reference to DataStorage and exposes methods for searching by patient or time range. Both DataStorage and DataRetriever depend on AccessControl before performing their operations, ensuring that only authorised roles can store/retrieve the given data. This structure was designed to abide by the SOLID principle of single responsibility, with each class owning one well-defined concern. It also partially satisfies the open-closed principle, as new retention rules or retrieval strategies can be introduced, for example by extending RetentionPolicy, without modifying unrelated classes.
